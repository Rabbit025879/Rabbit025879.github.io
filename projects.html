Robotics · Navigation DIT Robotics · 2024 – 2025

Eurobot Competition Robot

Problem

Eurobot's arena changes every year, but the constraint is constant: a robot has ~100 seconds to localize itself, plan around a dynamic opponent, and complete scoring actions — with dead-reckoning alone drifting too fast to trust.

Motivation

I wanted navigation, not just mechanics, to be where our team competed — and to lead the stack that our robot's performance actually hinged on match to match.

Architecture
LIDAR + Odometry → Localization → Nav2 Global/Local Planner → STM32 Motor Control

Custom costmap layers tracked the opposing robot as a dynamic obstacle, forcing continuous replanning rather than a single fixed path. Firmware on STM32 closed the low-level motor control loop in real time, decoupled from the higher-level ROS2 planning stack running on the onboard computer.

Testing

Full-arena rehearsals under match timing, logging localization drift and replanning latency across dozens of runs to find failure modes before they cost points on stage.

Challenges

Competition-hall lighting degraded sensor readings unpredictably, and every extra second spent re-localizing was a second not spent scoring — the stack had to be fast and forgiving at once.

Lessons Learned

Robust beats clever. A navigation stack with a boring, predictable failure mode wins more matches than one with a brilliant edge case it can't recover from — so I optimized for graceful degradation, not just best-case speed.

Mechatronics · Control National Competition · 2025

27th TDK Cup Entry

Problem

The TDK Cup poses an open-ended mechatronic design challenge each year: build a physical system, under strict rules and materials, that senses and responds to its environment reliably enough to demonstrate live in front of judges.

Motivation

A chance to close the loop from mechanical design to control theory to a working demo — outside the scope of the robotics team, on a much tighter build cycle.

Design Thinking & Implementation

Before committing to a mechanism, I modeled the control response in MATLAB to see how sensitive the design would be to real-world tolerances — cheaper to discover on a graph than in a prototype. The chosen design paired a sensor-driven feedback loop with a mechanical structure detailed in AutoCAD and Inventor, iterated through several rounds of bench prototyping to close the gap between simulated and real behavior.

Testing

Bench tests measured response time and repeatability against the modeled prediction, with control gains tuned iteratively until real-world behavior matched simulation.

Challenges

Fitting a reliable sensor and its wiring within a tight mechanical form factor, while keeping tolerances forgiving enough to survive a live demo in front of judges.

Result

Excellence & Creativity Award, 27th TDK Cup — recognized for the integration of sensing and control logic into a cohesive, well-reasoned design.

Lessons Learned

A design is only as good as its explanation. Judges respond to clear reasoning about trade-offs as much as the mechanism itself — documenting the "why" mattered as much as the build.

Sensors · Research Undergraduate Research · Sep 2024 – Dec 2025

CMOS-MEMS Pressure Sensor with Extended Sensing Range

Problem

Capacitive MEMS pressure sensors read high pressures well but lose sensitivity near vacuum; Pirani (thermal-conductivity) sensors do the opposite. Either alone leaves a gap in usable range.

Motivation

Wanted to see whether combining two mechanisms with complementary blind spots, on a single CMOS-MEMS die, could extend the usable range without a second discrete sensor.

Architecture & Implementation

Modeled the capacitive gap-sensing behavior alongside the Pirani element's thermal-conductivity response in COMSOL, then validated the combined readout circuit at the component level in LTspice — simulating both mechanisms before any fabrication commitment.

Capacitive Sensing (high P) + Pirani Sensing (low P) → Extended-Range Output
Testing

Simulated a full pressure sweep across the target range, comparing the combined-mechanism response curve against each mechanism modeled in isolation to quantify the coverage gain.

Challenges

Cross-talk between the two sensing mechanisms and thermal noise in the Pirani regime made a clean, calibratable combined signal harder to extract than either mechanism alone.

Results & Lessons Learned

The combined-mechanism model showed usable sensing coverage beyond what either mechanism achieved alone — the core hypothesis behind the design held up in simulation, setting up continued work as I move into graduate research at the NEAF Lab.

Simulation-first design pays for itself: catching cross-talk and calibration issues in COMSOL and LTspice is far cheaper than discovering them on a fabricated die.

Experiments Log

Smaller questions, worked through.

Not every experiment becomes a case study. This is the running log of smaller tests, tuning passes, and dead ends behind the work above.

2025-11 Robotics Costmap inflation tuning for opponent avoidance ⌄

Swept inflation-radius and cost-scaling parameters on the dynamic obstacle layer to find a setting that avoided the opposing robot without over-inflating paths around static walls.

→ Settled on a tighter inflation radius with a steeper cost falloff — fewer over-cautious detours.
2025-08 Sensors Combined-mechanism noise floor in LTspice ⌄

Measured simulated noise floor of the combined capacitive + Pirani readout circuit to see whether cross-talk between mechanisms would swamp the signal at the range boundary.

→ Noise stayed within an acceptable margin — worth carrying into the next simulation pass.
2025-05 Embedded STM32 control-loop jitter under load ⌄

Measured timing jitter on the motor control loop while the board was also handling serial communication with the onboard computer, to check the low-level loop stayed real-time.

→ Jitter increased under heavy serial load — moved communication to a lower-priority interrupt.
2025-02 Sensors Pirani element geometry sweep in COMSOL ⌄

Varied the thermal element's geometry across several configurations to see which shape gave the most linear response near the vacuum end of the target range.

→ A narrower element improved low-pressure linearity at a small cost to high-pressure range.
2024-12 Software Nav2 behavior trees for fallback recovery ⌄

Prototyped a custom behavior tree so the robot could fall back to a simpler recovery behavior when re-localization confidence dropped mid-match, instead of stalling.

→ Cut worst-case recovery time noticeably — adopted for the following season.
2024-09 Robotics AMCL vs. custom filter drift comparison ⌄

Compared localization drift over a full simulated match length between stock AMCL and a lighter custom particle filter tuned for the arena's known landmarks.

→ The custom filter drifted less over long matches; AMCL recovered faster from large jumps.
